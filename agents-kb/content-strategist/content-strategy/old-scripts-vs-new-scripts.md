# Old Scripts vs. New Scripts (What Used To Work vs. What Works Now)

**Source**: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcJ1DThRTPU/
**Category**: content-strategy
**Added**: 2026-08-18

## Core Insight
The scripts that used to go viral on social media no longer work. A "new script" upgrades six specific things over an "old script." Use it as a diagnostic upgrade checklist: for each dimension, stop doing the old version and switch to the new one.

## How It Works
Six old → new upgrades (framed as a side-by-side comparison):

1. **Hook.** Old: a verbal hook only. New: a *written + verbal + visual* hook (all three layered together).
2. **Content type.** Old: purely educational. New: a mix of *storytelling AND education*.
3. **Structure.** Old: no real structure. New: follows a defined *storytelling script structure* OR *educational script structure* (pick one deliberately).
4. **Value.** Old: fluffy, vague value. New: *measurable / specific* value.
5. **Filming format.** Old: filmed sitting down, talking to the camera. New: filmed with an *engaging format*.
6. **CTA (closer).** Ends on a lead-magnet CTA ("comment SCRIPT and I'll send the list of storytelling structures").

**Engaging formats shown on screen (the "new" filming options):** Voiceover, Talking Back & Forth, Reaction, Setting Changes, Whiteboard, Shot/Angle Change, Multitasking, Visual, Green Screen, Clone.

## Example
- **Fluffy vs. measurable value (fitness niche, shown on screen):** instead of vague "eat healthy, train hard," the measurable version is specific and countable: "Walk 10K steps/day · Eat in a 200-300 calorie deficit · Lift heavy 3-4x per week (Back & Biceps, Glutes & Hamstrings, Shoulder & Chest, Glutes & Quads) · Eat 1g of protein per pound you weigh daily."
- The reel itself is filmed as an "Old Scripts vs New Scripts" split/clone comparison (an engaging format), practicing what it teaches.

## When to Use
When auditing or rewriting a script that feels flat or "used to work but doesn't anymore." Run the video through all six dimensions and upgrade each old element to its new counterpart.
