# Golden Handcuffs vs. a Big Life That's Actually Yours

**Source**: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DavXRRNN0a-/ (also: https://www.instagram.com/p/DbYcNvhDvLw/)
**Category**: identity
**Added**: 2026-07-17

## Core Insight
There's a difference between a life that *looks* big to everyone around you and a life that's actually yours: the first is an impressive resume wrapped around golden handcuffs (which are not the same thing as financial freedom, they're a trap), while the second is intentionally designed around your own definition of success.

## How It Works
- **The test**: is your life designed intentionally by you, or does it just look impressive from the outside while making you feel trapped?
- **A big life that's yours** is built from total freedom: financial freedom, time freedom, and creative freedom. It means getting paid for being yourself and living out the dreams that matter most to you, whatever form that takes (a secluded cabin, a global stage, anything in between) - by your own definition of success.
- **A golden-handcuffs life** looks good on paper (impressive resume, good and comfortable circumstances) but isn't actually what you're meant for, and you've lost tolerance for it even though you've "done everything right."
- Knowing you're meant for more and dreaming about it isn't the same as doing something about it; the gap between talking about the big life and building it is where people get stuck.

## Example
"Your resume is impressive as hell and you've got golden handcuffs, which golden handcuffs are not synonymous with financial freedom. They're a trap." Framed against the alternative: "Whether that's a secluded cabin in the forest, or being on stage making a global impact, it's building a life that matters by your definition of success."

## When to Use
When a multi-passionate client is objectively "doing well" (good job, impressive title, comfortable life) but feels trapped or has lost tolerance for it. Use as a diagnostic: is this life designed by you, or does it just look good to others? Pairs with [[what-if-you-went-bigger]] (same impressive-but-unfulfilled paradox) and [[permission-to-be-big]] (permission to stop shrinking into a life that isn't yours).
