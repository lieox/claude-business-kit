# The Big Life Self-Audit: 4 Questions If You Keep Talking About Your Dreams But Not Acting

**Source**: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcQ70zeOaer/
**Category**: mindset
**Added**: 2026-08-21

## Core Insight
Building the big life is 20% strategy and 80% self-belief, so if you keep talking about your dreams but never move, the block is almost never tactical, it is that you are protecting yourself from uncomfortable feelings. Four brutally honest questions surface the avoidance, fake-confusion, hidden costs, and victim patterns that keep you playing small.

## How It Works
Ask yourself these 4 questions and answer with radical honesty (the on-screen headers number them; the spoken hook frames them as "10 out of 10 questions to ask yourself"):

- **1. What am I avoiding?** Which uncomfortable feelings are you dodging by not taking action? Usually a fear: of finding out you don't have what it takes, of being seen, of rejection, of failure, or even of success. Staying small feels protective, it means you never go out on a limb and expose yourself to what your nervous system processes as a life-or-death threat.
- **2. What am I pretending to be confused about that I actually have clarity on?** People on the cusp of radically changing their lives often perform "utter confusion," but when pressed they already know the answer. Confusion is a permission slip not to act, you block the clarity so you are excused from moving.
- **3. What is it costing me?** Name the real, daily price of staying in place. You have normalized the consequences so much you have stopped clocking them (e.g. stuck in "500-view jail" as a creator because you won't do the scary growth moves; carrying credit card debt for 15 years because the stress becomes the excuse to "play it safe"). You have decided the current pain is more tolerable than the discomfort of putting yourself out there, which means you are actively keeping yourself in a life you do not want.
- **4. How am I victimizing myself?** Where do you complain and call yourself a failure after trying for only a little while without fast results? Playing the victim makes quitting (or never starting) feel justified, and underneath it is the fantasy that success should have magically happened. It never magically happens. You have to do the work.

The through-line: the pursuit of a big life *requires* confronting the shit you are hiding from, there is no way around it, and you do it scared.

## Example
Two concrete costs she names for Question 3: (a) a would-be content creator stuck getting only 500 views because they refuse to do the few growth actions that scare them; (b) someone carrying credit card debt for 15 years despite making good money, because the ongoing stress of the debt supplies a ready excuse to stay the course and not leap to the bigger, more successful life.

## When to Use
When you keep announcing a goal but not executing, feel "stuck" or "confused" about an obviously-known next step, or catch yourself in complaint/victim mode about slow results. Run it as a journaling or coaching prompt to expose the emotional block behind the inaction before touching strategy.
