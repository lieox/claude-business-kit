# Courage Is Chaotic - The Truth About Leaving Corporate

**Source**: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DZLpyTeN53c/
**Category**: mindset
**Added**: 2026-06-09

## Core Insight
We picture making the big leap from a place of total confidence, but that's not what courage looks like. "Courage is chaotic": exciting one moment, "oh shit, what have I done" the next. Betting on yourself is terrifying and messy, and it can still be the best decision you ever make.

## How It Works
- **The leap feels like terror, not confidence.** When Annie left, she'd just made irreversible, committed decisions and was "fucking terrified", white-knuckling it. Courage isn't calm; it's chaotic and contradictory.
- **Freedom is immediate.** The moment you slam the laptop shut for the last time, all the BS that weighed on you for so long is gone, and you get to put 100% of your energy toward building your future.
- **Grief is part of it.** The day she left was a weird mix: deep peace *and* unexpected grief for a life that wasn't making her happy but that she'd spent 13 years building, plus the stark awareness that she's now responsible for her own livelihood.
- **The real prize is who you become.** Five years on, she's most in awe not of the business but of the person she's become, fully reclaimed identity, creativity, audacity, vibrance, and confidence.
- **Your rejected parts become your asset.** The gift is to use the parts of you that were once rejected as your greatest money-making asset, the things that make you most magnetic and attract your right people.

## Example
On her 5-year anniversary of leaving corporate, Annie drops the "profound lessons" hook and has a raw conversation instead: damn proud of the business, would "choose this hard every single time," and "just getting started." Honest caveat: it's scary, hard, and not for everyone, but if you're the right fit, it's the most incredible journey.

## When to Use
When someone is paralyzed waiting to feel *confident* before leaving a job / making the leap, reframe the standard: courage is supposed to feel chaotic and scary. Normalize the terror and the grief, point to the immediate freedom and the identity you reclaim on the other side.
