# Cringe Mountain: It's Only Cringe Until It's Cool

**Source**: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcMerLZtZ-a/
**Category**: mindset
**Added**: 2026-08-19

## Core Insight
The reason a visionary hasn't chased the "something big" they know they're meant for is that going big requires climbing "cringe mountain": showing up before anyone sees your vision, before anyone claps, while people reject you and call you "too much." The discomfort ("cringe") is not a stop sign, it's a normal, expected part of the process, because anything that ends up cool starts out cringe. It only feels scary because other people are more comfortable when you stay small and predictable.

## How It Works
- **Name the identity:** you've always known you're meant for something big; you're the multi-passionate, idea-bursting visionary who's been "too big, too expansive, too inquisitive, too curious" for every role you've held.
- **Diagnose why you're stuck:** you haven't explored the big thing because the stakes feel high, and the first step is climbing cringe mountain, showing up when the arena is empty and no one is clapping.
- **Reframe the fear:** going big only feels scary because other people are comfortable when you're small, predictable, easy to handle, and easy to label. The fear is about their comfort, not your capability.
- **Reframe what people actually need:** people think they want comfort, but what they really need is change, disruption, innovation, and art that shakes up their worldview. That's how evolution happens, individually and collectively.
- **Accept the toll as normal:** you have to move through the cringe for what you create to become cool. Knowing that's a normal part of the process lets you "embrace the hard" instead of reading discomfort as failure.
- **Take responsibility:** as a visionary you're responsible for putting the disruption out there and being the leader/future-shaper you're meant to be.

## Example
Annie's framing line, repeated as bookends: "It's only cringe until it's cool." She paints the cost concretely, "you have to show up when no one else sees your vision, when the arena's empty and no one's clapping for you, when people reject you, when they tell you you're too much", then flips it: "people think they want comfort. What they really need is change." She closes on the reassurance that the cringe is normal ("that's a totally normal part of the process") plus her usual offer to help craft the vision and mindset to go big.

## When to Use
When a multi-passionate/visionary client is stalling on a big move (launching, publishing, going public with an idea) because it feels cringe, embarrassing, or premature, and no one is validating them yet. Use it to normalize the discomfort of being early and unseen, and to reframe "too much" rejection as evidence they're disrupting rather than a signal to shrink. Pairs with [[permission-to-be-big]] (the "too much" reframe) and [[courage-is-chaotic-leaving-corporate]] (fear is part of the leap, not proof you're wrong).
