# Your Inner Knowing Won't Let You Forget: Why Staying Stuck Costs More Than Leaping

**Source**: https://www.instagram.com/p/DcO_UTEGnPw/
**Category**: mindset
**Added**: 2026-08-20

## Core Insight
When you're a multi-passionate meant to build something of your own, the fear of what you might LOSE by leaping keeps beating your focus on what you stand to GAIN, so you stay stuck. But your inner knowing won't let you forget: that core part of you has already decided you're going after it, ready or not. You either act on it, or you die with regret. The tipping point comes only when staying becomes more painful than the risk of going.

## How It Works
- **Loss-aversion keeps winning.** "The part of me that wouldn't stop worrying about what I might lose if I leapt kept WINNING. I couldn't fully lean into what I stood to GAIN." Staying stuck isn't a logic problem, it's fear of loss overriding the upside.
- **The "just be grateful" trap.** You have one okay-ish moment at work and immediately find an excuse to back out: "maybe my job's not that bad, maybe I should just be grateful." That trope sounds noble but it's really just permission to keep hiding.
- **Staying is more expensive than leaping.** Being mentally and emotionally checked out of a job while physically still there is "a special kind of hell": you're masking, you lose tolerance for everyone and every to-do, everything pisses you off. Staying in that mode long-term is way more energetically expensive than chasing your dreams.
- **The gain that should make you MOVE.** You outperform everyone, you're too big a fish in too small a pond, sought-after and a high earner, but not recognized for what actually makes you excellent (your creativity, range, innovative ideas, visionary spirit). That should piss you off in a good way, the way that makes you want to move.
- **The tipping point.** The leap happens when "staying HAS to be more painful than the risk of going for it. I have to believe in myself." The more you let yourself SEE your own value, the less you can stand wasting it.

## Example
Annie's own arc: full meltdowns over changing her life in 2020, repeating the maddening loop for years, until June 4, 2021, the day she "slammed the corporate laptop shut for the LAST TIME." She made the decision, leapt a year later, and hasn't looked back "not even for a second." The receipts (dated photos of her freaking out, then leaving) prove the stuck phase felt permanent right up until it broke.

## When to Use
When a multi-passionate is objectively successful but stuck, and keeps rationalizing staying ("I should just be grateful", "maybe it's not that bad"). Diagnose it as loss-aversion beating gain-focus, name the gratitude trope as a hiding mechanism, and reframe the real cost: staying stuck is more draining than leaping. The lever is not more confidence, it's making the pain of staying exceed the risk of going.
