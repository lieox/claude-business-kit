## Core Insight
"Lock in" and "obsession is the key to success" content is aimed at people who have never actually locked in a day in their life. High-achieving, overthinking creatives already over-index on obsessive focus, to their own detriment, and it is the obsession itself (not a lack of it) that burns them out and dries up their creativity. Annie names the alternative "Funmaxxing": maximizing enjoyment in everything (work and life) as the actual growth mechanism, not a reward for growth.

## How It Works
- Setting a big goal already makes you obsessive by default; you don't need more of it, you need less
- Over-focusing on one goal blocks everything else out and shrinks your world, you say "no" to anything that isn't work
- If you're not actively doing the work, you're problem-solving about it in your mind, there's no real off switch
- This pours you out until there's nothing left: dry creativity well, burnout, resentment toward your own goal, and you're still unsatisfied even if you hit it
- Joy, fulfillment, and massive success don't happen in the hustle, they happen in FLOW: play, challenges that excite you, sparked curiosity, learning something new
- You thrive in AWE and FUN, not in white-knuckling productivity
- **Funmaxxing test**: if a task doesn't feel like play, spark curiosity, expand your world, or challenge you the right way, don't do it
- **Why it works (the mechanism)**: when grinding, business and wellbeing both suffer; when over-indexing on fun instead, both thrive, because enjoyment stops the overthinking loop. The stop-overthinking state is described as "superhuman": magnetic, unstoppable, idea-generating, visionary, "genius just pours out" - that state is where the money comes from, not the grind
- Pairs with a public accountability frame: state the concrete goal (e.g. "$1M in one year"), name the mechanism by which you'll hit it (fun, not hustle), and commit publicly to documenting the attempt in real time
- **Why creativity gets suppressed in the first place**: you're not taught to lightly set creativity aside, you're taught to abandon it entirely, with the fear of "the corporate gods" struck into you (get serious, stop being whimsical, creativity won't make you money) - because an unleashed, creative, flow-state version of you is too powerful to stay small, exploited, or underpaid
- Fun isn't just an idea-generator, it's also where trust and possibility come from; when you're in flow you show up as your most unapologetic, confident self, and that version of you won't stay small or keep serving someone else's bottom line
- **The causal chain, made explicit (carousel 5):** fun is not the money source itself. Fun is "the most necessary and foundational ingredient to ignite the thing that actually generates abundance": your **unique genius** (see [[unique-genius-magic-sauce-triple-threat]]). Her confidence in the $1M bet rests on two things she knows "better than anything": (1) exactly what her genius is, and (2) that FUN is what unlocks it. When she operates from that zone, the best ideas arrive, she becomes "magnetic, undeniable", and "THAT'S when profit explodes."
- **Peaks-vs-valleys audit (the evidence move):** hold your highest peaks against your deepest valleys and look for the one pattern. Hers, across 6+ years in business: every low was a time she was "an overworking, overthinking, chaotic mess", sucked into hustle culture, "gripping my goals so hard I squeezed the life out of them", spiraling into burnout (2024); every high was a time she was "enjoying the hell out of my life", letting adventures inspire the work: "joy fed creativity, creativity compounded."
- **Why most people stay stuck grinding:** they never figure out what their genius is, or they don't trust it, "and that's why they stay stuck grinding instead of expanding."

## When to Use
When a high-achieving, overthinking creative is stuck in grind mode, burned out, or creatively dry despite (or because of) working harder on their big goal, reframe the fix as adding play/curiosity/wonder back in, not doubling down on more obsessive focus. Use the "Funmaxxing" framing specifically when the ask is about a concrete growth goal (revenue target, launch) and the person needs a named, declarable mechanism (not just a vague "have more fun") plus a public-commitment structure. When someone asks why fun would produce money, use the two-part confidence formula (know what your genius is + know what unlocks it) and run the peaks-vs-valleys audit on their own history before prescribing anything.
