# The Opportunity-Hijack Cycle: Why You Keep Not Building Your Own Thing

**Source**: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdHE4ZYOkLK/
**Category**: mindset
**Added**: 2026-09-14

## Core Insight
High-achieving multi-passionates who want to build their own thing get stuck in a repeating cycle: they get fired up to finally go for their own vision, then someone taps them for a new, tangible, well-paid opportunity, they hedge and take it "because the money is really good," and their own goal gets pushed aside again. The fix isn't more motivation, it's admitting the cycle isn't working and that you're wired for entrepreneurship, not employment.

## How It Works
- **The setup**: you've already achieved conventional success, you make great money, and you've built a reputation as the go-to person who can solve any problem, so opportunities never stop coming.
- **The itch**: you think about building your own thing constantly, you visualize what freedom would feel like, and you're sick of bringing other people's goals to life instead of your own.
- **The double bind**: you're also sick of being the sought-after high performer while feeling undervalued, because you're barely scratching the surface of your real potential.
- **The spark**: you get fired up and commit, "I'm finally going to figure out my vision and go for it."
- **The hijack**: right on cue, someone taps you for a new opportunity. The gusto tempers immediately, you hedge ("maybe I should just say yes, the money is really good, I know I can crush it"), and because it's tangible (unlike your own still-unbuilt vision) you take it.
- **The reset**: your own goal gets pushed aside again, and the cycle repeats until you consciously name it.
- **The confrontation question**: how many times will you repeat this before admitting it isn't bringing the results, fulfillment, or freedom you actually want, and that you're wired for entrepreneurship, not employment?

## Example
Annie names the exact internal dialogue in the moment of hijack: "Well, maybe I should just say yes. The money is really good. I know I can crush it." That's the tell, the opportunity wins not because it's better than the vision, but because it's tangible and the vision isn't built yet.

## When to Use
When a client keeps saying "this time I'll finally build my own thing" and then gets derailed every single time by a new lucrative gig or ask, diagnose it as this specific cycle (not a motivation problem, not a bandwidth problem): the tangible opportunity always beats the not-yet-real vision unless the vision gets built into something tangible too.
