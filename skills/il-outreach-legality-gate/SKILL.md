# Israel Outreach Legality Gate

## Overview

A fast, self-contained checklist to run BEFORE committing to any outbound or marketing-channel plan aimed at Israeli recipients. It does not replace a lawyer; it stops the one mistake that has a real, documented lawsuit history: sending unsolicited marketing (email or SMS) to people who never opted in.

The rule exists because it already bit: a cold-batch prospect threatened a spam lawsuit and the exposure was confirmed real, forcing a pivot away from cold email to a consent-based paid funnel. Treat this gate as mandatory friction, not advice.

The governing law is the Israeli Communications Law (Telecommunications and Broadcasting), section 30a, Amendment 40 (חוק התקשורת (בזק ושידורים), ס' 30א, תיקון 40).

## When to Use

Run this gate when a plan, task, agent, or script proposes any of:

- Cold email or cold SMS to prospects or strangers.
- A bulk email blast, newsletter, drip/nurture sequence, or WhatsApp broadcast.
- Loading or buying a lead list and messaging it.
- Any "reach out to these people" step where you cannot point to their prior opt-in.

If any marketing message will land in an inbox/phone of someone who did not explicitly ask for it, the gate applies.

## When NOT to Use (or when it passes trivially)

- Purely inbound or organic work: SEO, content on your own channels, a public landing page.
- Paid ads (the recipient is not being messaged directly; they see an ad).
- Continuing a conversation with someone who already replied "yes" / engaged: a reply to existing outreach is a warm, consented lead and is fine to continue.
- Transactional/service messages to an active customer that are not marketing (e.g. an order or appointment notice). Note the marketing "existing customer" exception is narrow (see below); do not stretch it into cold outreach.

## The Gate (run top to bottom)

1. **Is this marketing (davar pirsomet), and is the recipient Israeli?**
   If not marketing, or not an Israeli recipient, this gate does not block it (other laws may still apply; out of scope here).

2. **Did every recipient give documented prior opt-in consent?**
   - YES for all recipients -> proceed to step 4 (message-content requirements).
   - NO / unknown / "we found their email" -> the channel is likely illegal. Go to step 3.

3. **The channel is cold. Pick a permitted channel instead, or stop.**
   Do NOT send cold email or cold SMS. Permitted alternatives:
   - Paid ads (Google, Meta, and similar).
   - A documented opt-in list (people who explicitly subscribed; keep proof of consent).
   - Manual, one-to-one LinkedIn messages (a personal DM, not a scripted mass blast).
   - Phone outreach.
   - Content marketing (publish and let people come inbound).
   Re-plan on one of these, then re-enter the gate.

4. **Message-content requirements (for permitted, consented sends).**
   Every marketing message must include all of:
   - The word **פרסומת** (Hebrew for "advertisement").
   - The advertiser's **name and address**.
   - A **one-click unsubscribe** link/mechanism (and honor removals).
   Missing any of these makes even a consented send non-compliant.

5. **Escalate to real legal review** (do not self-clear) when:
   - You want to rely on the "existing customer" exception, or any consent edge case.
   - Consent exists but is old, implied, bundled, or otherwise not a clean explicit opt-in.
   - The campaign is high-volume, cross-border, or the client is unsure of their consent records.
   - Anyone has threatened, or hinted at, a spam complaint or lawsuit.
   For anything beyond the clear-cut cases above, hand off to proper legal review rather than guessing.

## Why This Is Not Optional

- Statutory damages run up to **₪1,000 per message**, and the claimant does **not** need to prove any actual damage. A single cold batch can therefore create large aggregate exposure fast.
- The "existing customer" marketing exception is **narrow** and does **not** cover cold outreach to prospects/strangers. Do not treat "they might be interested" as consent.
- Cold email to strangers is a violation on its face. When in doubt, it is illegal, not "gray".

## Fast Verdict Table

| Situation | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Cold email/SMS to a bought or scraped list | BLOCK. Re-plan on a permitted channel. |
| Email/SMS to a documented opt-in list, with פרסומת + sender name/address + unsubscribe | OK to send. |
| Reply/nurture to someone who already engaged or said yes | OK (warm, consented). |
| Paid ad campaign (no direct message to a private inbox/phone) | OK (out of this gate's block scope). |
| Manual one-to-one LinkedIn DM | OK (permitted channel). |
| "Existing customer" exception, old/implied consent, or any doubt | ESCALATE to legal review. |

## Applying This Across Clients and Agents

- This gate is client-agnostic. It applies to any account, campaign, or list. Use neutral placeholders when documenting a plan (a service business, `<Client>`, `{campaignId}`), never a specific client's records.
- Any agent or task that plans outreach or drafts marketing sends (outbound, email/drip, social DM, SMS/WhatsApp) should run this gate before proposing a send, and route hard cases to a dedicated legal reviewer.
- The safe default when consent is unclear is to switch channels (paid ads / consented list / manual LinkedIn / phone / content), not to send and hope.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Treating "we can reach them" (found their email) as permission | Only documented, explicit prior opt-in counts. No opt-in -> no cold send. |
| Stretching the "existing customer" exception to cover prospects | The exception is narrow and excludes cold outreach; escalate instead of assuming. |
| Sending a consented blast without פרסומת / sender name+address / unsubscribe | All three are mandatory on every marketing message, even to opt-in lists. |
| Assuming a small batch is low-risk | Damages are up to ₪1,000 per message with no proof-of-damage needed; volume multiplies exposure. |
| Ignoring a spam-complaint threat and continuing | Stop the send and escalate to legal review immediately. |
